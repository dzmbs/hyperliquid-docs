### Trademark markings and related issues

Hyper Foundation is the owner of the name and trademarks HYPERLIQUID, HYPE, HYPURR, and a blob logo (both in static and in motion forms), appearing alone and in conjunction with other words, and is also the owner of the look and feel of goods and services offered under its trademarks, inclusive of logos, designs, color(s), names, typeface(s), and other elements that serve to distinguish Hyper Foundation from others (collectively, the “Hyperliquid Marks”), and all uses of the Hyperliquid Marks are subject to the Hyper Foundation Trademark Usage and Licensing Guidelines (“Guidelines”). This portion of the Brand Kit is incorporated by reference into the Guidelines and is intended to clarify and provide additional explanations for some of the issues discussed in the Guidelines.

All uses of the Hyperliquid Marks must feature appropriate trademark markings, such as HYPERLIQUIDTM or HYPERLIQUID®. The ® marking should only be used in jurisdictions where the mark is registered, while all other uses should feature the TM marking. The below table identifies where the Hyperliquid Marks are registered. If you reside in one of those jurisdictions or are hosting an in-person event in a relevant jurisdiction, you should use the ® marking. This table may be updated from time to time, and you agree to update your usage of the Hyperliquid Marks accordingly.

As required by the Guidelines, you must use the Hyperliquid Marks as brand identifiers (adjectives or nouns) and not generically, as a descriptive term for products or services, or as a verb. So, for example, stating that you’re hosting an app on the HYPERLIQUIDTM blockchain would be an acceptable use of the term as a proper noun. While stating that your app is hosted on a blockchain that is hyperliquid would be using the term descriptively, as it indicates a feature of the blockchain and does not use the term to identify the source of the blockchain. Further, stating that your app allows users to “hyperliquid your trades” or interact with a “hyperliquid market” would also be improper uses of the term as a verb or generic term.
